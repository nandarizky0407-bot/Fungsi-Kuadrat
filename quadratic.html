Material 01 of 10

What are quadratic expressions?

Definition

A quadratic expression is a polynomial of degree 2 — the highest power of the variable is 2.

ax² + bx + c

where a ≠ 0, and a, b, c are constants.

Examples vs Non-Examples

Quadratic
3x² + 5x − 2
Has x² term; a=3, b=5, c=−2
Quadratic
x² − 9
b=0, c=−9 (still quadratic)
Not Quadratic
5x + 3
Linear — highest power is 1

Key Insight

The word "quadratic" comes from quadratus (Latin for "square") — because x² represents the area of a square with side x.

Identifying a, b, c

Example 1
2x² + 7x − 3
a = 2, b = 7, c = −3
Example 2
4x²
a = 4, b = 0, c = 0
Example 3
x² − 9
a = 1, b = 0, c = −9
Check

If there's no x² term (or a=0), it's NOT quadratic. The degree must be exactly 2.

How to add quadratic expressions

The Principle

To add quadratic expressions, combine like terms — terms that share the same variable raised to the same power.

(ax² + bx + c) + (dx² + ex + f)
= (a+d)x² + (b+e)x + (c+f)

Like Terms Reminder

Same Power
3x² and 5x²
Both have x² — they can be combined
Different Power
3x² and 5x
x² ≠ x — they cannot combine

Worked Example

Add: (2x² + 3x + 1) + (x² + 4x + 5)

Step 1 — Group like terms
(2x² + x²) + (3x + 4x) + (1 + 5)
Step 2 — Combine
3x² + 7x + 6
Final Answer
3x² + 7x + 6
Pro Tip

Stack the expressions vertically — like ordinary addition — to keep like terms aligned.

Material 03 of 10

How to subtract quadratic expressions

The Principle

The most common mistake is forgetting to distribute the negative sign to every term inside the second bracket.

(ax² + bx + c) − (dx² + ex + f)
= (a−d)x² + (b−e)x + (c−f)

Common Trap

Wrong
5x² − (2x² + 3x) ≠ 5x² − 2x² + 3x
Forgot to negate 3x!
Right
5x² − (2x² + 3x) = 5x² − 2x² − 3x = 3x² − 3x

Worked Example

Subtract: (5x² + 3x − 2) − (2x² + x + 4)

Step 1 — Distribute the negative
5x² + 3x − 2 − 2x² − x − 4
Step 2 — Group like terms
(5x² − 2x²) + (3x − x) + (−2 − 4)
Step 3 — Combine
3x² + 2x − 6
Memory Hook

The minus sign is like a "wave" — it must touch every term in the bracket.

Material 04 of 10

How to expand quadratic expressions

The Distributive Law

Expanding means removing brackets by multiplying each term inside by the term outside.

a(b + c) = ab + ac
a(b + c + d) = ab + ac + ad

Expanding with a Variable Outside

Example
3(x² + 2x + 4)
Multiply each term
= 3·x² + 3·2x + 3·4
Simplify
= 3x² + 6x + 12

Worked Example

Expand: 2x(x + 5)

Step 1 — Identify
2x is outside; x and 5 are inside
Step 2 — Distribute
2x · x + 2x · 5
Step 3 — Multiply
2x² + 10x
Laws of Indices

When multiplying like bases: xᵏ × xⁿ = xᵏ⁺ⁿ

How to factorise quadratic expressions

The Inverse of Expanding

Factorising rewrites a sum as a product. We look for the highest common factor (HCF).

ab + ac = a(b + c)

Step-by-Step Method

Step 1
Find HCF of coefficients
Step 2
Find HCF of variables (lowest power)
Step 3
Write HCF outside; divide each term

Worked Example

Factorise: 4x² + 8x

HCF of 4 and 8
→ 4
HCF of x² and x
→ x (lowest power)
Combined HCF
→ 4x
Factorise
4x² + 8x = 4x(x + 2)
Check Your Answer

Always expand your factorised answer back — if you get the original, you're correct!

Material 06 of 10

How to simplify quadratic expressions

What "Simplify" Means

Simplifying reduces an expression to its cleanest form. Strategies include:

  • Combining like terms
  • Removing brackets (expanding)
  • Cancelling common factors
  • Factorising when useful

Strategy Examples

Combine like terms
3x² + 5x² − 2x = 8x² − 2x
Cancel common factors
(6x² + 9x) ÷ 3 = 2x² + 3x

Worked Example

Simplify: 3(x + 2) + 2(x + 2)

Method A — Expand first
3x + 6 + 2x + 4
Combine
5x + 10
Method B — Factorise (faster!)
(3 + 2)(x + 2) = 5(x + 2)
Smart Simplification

Always look for shared brackets first — it can save you several steps.

Material 07 of 10

Simplifying in two or more variables

Like Terms in Multiple Variables

Terms are "like" only when all variables and their powers match exactly.

3x²y ~ 5x²y ✓
3x²y ~ 3xy² ✗

Multiplying Variables

Add exponents of same base
x² · x³ = x⁵
Different bases stay separate
a² · b³ = a²b³
Power of a power
(x²)³ = x⁶

Worked Example

Simplify: (2a²b³)(3ab²)

Multiply coefficients
2 × 3 = 6
Multiply a's (add powers)
a² · a = a³
Multiply b's (add powers)
b³ · b² = bⁱ
Final Answer
6a³bⁱ
Visual Memory Hook

Think of a²b³ as "a-a-b-b-b". Multiplying by ab² adds one more a and two more b's.

Material 08 of 10

Expanding (a+b)(c+d)

The FOIL Method

When multiplying two binomials, multiply each term of the first by each term of the second.

(a+b)(c+d) = ac + ad + bc + bd

First · Outside · Inside · Last

Visual: The Area Model

Imagine a rectangle split into 4 parts
(a+b)(c+d) = area of 4 smaller rectangles
= ac + ad + bc + bd

Worked Example

Expand: (x + 3)(x + 5)

F — First
x · x = x²
O — Outside
x · 5 = 5x
I — Inside
3 · x = 3x
L — Last
3 · 5 = 15
Combine like terms
x² + 8x + 15

Material 09 of 10

Factorising by grouping

The Method

When an expression has four terms with no common factor across all of them, we group them in pairs.

ac + ad + bc + bd
= a(c+d) + b(c+d)
= (a+b)(c+d)

Three Steps

Step 1
Group into two pairs
Step 2
Factorise each pair separately
Step 3
Factor out the common bracket

Worked Example

Factorise: 2x² + 6x + 3x + 9

Step 1 — Group pairs
(2x² + 6x) + (3x + 9)
Step 2 — Factorise each pair
2x(x + 3) + 3(x + 3)
Step 3 — Common bracket is (x+3)
(2x + 3)(x + 3)
Final Answer
(2x + 3)(x + 3)
Watch For

If the brackets don't match after Step 2, you may need to swap terms or factor out a negative.

Material 10 of 10

Special identities & tricks

The Three Famous Identities

(a+b)² = a² + 2ab + b²
(a−b)² = a² − 2ab + b²
a² − b² = (a+b)(a−b)

Why They Matter

Speed
Skip the FOIL steps — recognise the pattern and write the answer directly.
Reverse use
Factorise instantly: x² − 16 = (x+4)(x−4)

Worked Examples

Identity 1 → Expand (x+4)²
= x² + 2(4)x + 4²
= x² + 8x + 16
Identity 2 → Expand (2x−3)²
= (2x)² − 2(2x)(3) + 3²
= 4x² − 12x + 9
Identity 3 → Factorise x² − 25
a² = x², b² = 25 → b = 5
= (x+5)(x−5)
Memory Hooks

Square of sum: "Square, Double, Square"
Difference of squares: "Minus means plus-times-minus"
